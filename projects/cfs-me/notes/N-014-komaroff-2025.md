# Candidate causes and chronicity mechanisms in the 2025 review

## Summary
This 2025 review discusses causes of symptoms and their persistence in long COVID and ME/CFS, distinguishing symptom mechanisms from a single root cause. It says ME/CFS is likely often a post-acute infection syndrome, triggered by multiple different infectious agents, and says there is little evidence for one novel infectious agent causing ME/CFS. It describes candidate or contributing abnormalities including persistent infection or molecular residues, tissue injury and inflammation, reactivation of latent herpesviruses and other agents, gut dysbiosis, autoantibodies, endothelial dysfunction, mitochondrial dysfunction, autonomic dysfunction and redox imbalance. The authors propose that neuroinflammation can activate brain circuits generating sickness symptoms and torpor-like responses, and that interacting abnormalities can sustain illness; many claims are explicitly framed as likely, plausible or requiring confirmation.
